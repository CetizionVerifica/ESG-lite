ESGLite sorts emissions into three scopes, as the GHG Protocol does. Every category belongs to one scope, and you see the scope next to the category name in Add data and Current Month.

**Scope 1: direct emissions**
From sources your company owns or controls. Typical examples: fuel burned in boilers, furnaces, generators and company vehicles, and gases leaking from refrigeration or air-conditioning.

**Scope 2: purchased energy**
From the electricity, heat, steam or cooling your company buys. The emissions happen where the energy is made, but they count for you because you use it.

**Scope 3: the value chain**
Everything else your activities cause outside your own operations. The GHG Protocol splits it into 15 categories, such as purchased goods and services, business travel, employee commuting, waste, transport, and use of sold products. Your company decides which of them it reports.

**tCO₂e**
Results are in tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO₂e). Other greenhouse gases are counted as the amount of CO₂ with the same warming effect, so everything adds up on one scale.

**Categories with no scope**
Some categories, such as renewable electricity your company produces, have no scope. Reports show them as emissions saved instead of adding them to a scope.

**Where scopes appear**
- [Current Month](/my-month) groups your categories by scope.
- Reports and the Overview show totals for Scope 1, 2 and 3.

If you're not sure which category an activity belongs in, ask your manager. ESGLite only shows you the categories you've been given.
